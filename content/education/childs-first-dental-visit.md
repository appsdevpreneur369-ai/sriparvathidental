A first dental visit should be short, friendly and nothing like the stories children sometimes hear. Starting early helps it stay that way.

## When to come

Many dentists suggest a first visit when the first teeth appear, or by about your child's first birthday. Coming before there is any problem means the first experience is a pleasant one.

## Before the visit

- Talk about the dentist in a matter-of-fact, positive way. Avoid words like "hurt" or "injection".
- Choose a time when your child is usually rested and fed.
- Read a picture book or play "dentist" at home, counting teeth with a toothbrush.
- Bring any questions about thumb-sucking, teething or night feeds.

## On the day

For young children the visit is mostly about getting comfortable. They meet the dentist, try the chair (often on a parent's lap), and the dentist counts the teeth and looks for early signs of cavities. You'll get advice on brushing, toothpaste and diet suited to your child's age.

## Habits that protect milk teeth

- **Brush twice a day** with a smear of fluoride toothpaste for under-threes and a pea-sized amount after that. Help with brushing until about age seven or eight.
- **Limit sugary snacks and drinks** to mealtimes rather than through the day.
- **Avoid putting a child to bed with a bottle** of milk or juice.
- **Keep regular check-ups**, usually every six months unless advised otherwise.

Milk teeth hold the space for permanent teeth and help children chew and speak, so they are worth looking after — even though they fall out.
