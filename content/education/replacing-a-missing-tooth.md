Losing a tooth is more than a gap in your smile. Over time, nearby teeth can tip into the space, the opposite tooth can over-erupt, and chewing shifts to one side. Replacing the tooth helps prevent this. There are three main options.

## Dental implant

A small titanium post is placed in the jaw where the root used to be. Once it has bonded with the bone — usually over a few months — a crown is fixed on top.

- **Feels and works** most like a natural tooth
- **Does not involve** the neighbouring teeth
- **Needs** enough healthy bone and gums, and a few months from start to finish

## Fixed bridge

A false tooth is held in place by crowns on the teeth either side of the gap.

- **Fixed in place** and usually completed in two or three visits
- **Needs** the neighbouring teeth to be shaped for crowns, which suits best when they already have large fillings
- **Cleaning** under the false tooth needs a little extra care

## Removable denture

A plate carries one or more false teeth and clips on to the remaining teeth (partial) or rests on the gums (complete).

- **Often the simplest and most affordable** option, especially for several missing teeth
- **Removable**, so it is taken out for cleaning and at night
- **Takes some getting used to** and may need adjusting over time

## Which is right for you?

The best choice depends on how many teeth are missing, the health of the teeth and gums around the gap, the bone, your general health and your budget.
