Ask people what they fear most at the dentist and "root canal" usually tops the list. Yet the pain people remember is most often the toothache that came *before* treatment — the treatment itself is designed to end it.

## Why a tooth needs a root canal

Inside every tooth is a soft core called the pulp, containing nerves and blood vessels. Deep decay, a crack or a knock can let bacteria reach it. The pulp becomes inflamed or infected, and because it is trapped inside hard tooth, the pressure builds. That is the throbbing, sleep-stealing ache many people describe.

Common warning signs include:

- pain that lingers long after something hot or cold
- toothache that wakes you at night
- pain on biting, or a tooth that feels "high"
- a pimple-like bump or swelling on the gum near one tooth

Sometimes a tooth needs treatment with no symptoms at all, which is one reason regular check-ups matter.

## What happens during treatment

1. **Numbing.** The tooth and gum are numbed with local anaesthetic. Most people feel pressure and vibration, not pain.
2. **Access and cleaning.** A small opening is made in the top of the tooth. The infected pulp is removed and the narrow canals are cleaned, shaped and disinfected.
3. **Sealing.** The canals are filled and sealed so bacteria cannot get back in. Depending on the tooth this can be done in one visit or spread over two or three.
4. **Protecting the tooth.** Back teeth usually need a crown afterwards, because a root-treated tooth is more likely to crack.

## How it feels afterwards

Tenderness when biting for a few days is common and usually settles with simple painkillers your dentist recommends. Chew on the other side until the final filling or crown is in place.

> Call the clinic if pain gets worse after two or three days, if swelling appears, or if a temporary filling falls out.

## The alternative

The alternative to a root canal is usually removing the tooth. That can be the right choice in some cases, but a gap brings its own problems — neighbouring teeth drift, chewing shifts to one side, and replacing the tooth later costs more. Saving a natural tooth is often worth it.
